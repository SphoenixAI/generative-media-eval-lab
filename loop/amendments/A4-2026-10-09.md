## 1. Why this track exists

SC3-Eval (Tseng et al., arXiv 2606.18610, June 2026) earns trust in a video world model with three consistency checks, and maps each onto the lab:

| SC3-Eval check | What it does | Lab equivalent | Items |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forward-inverse | Predicts frames from actions, and recovers actions from frames | Clip to intent and back; a judge's stated reason, tested by editing the factor it names | WP06, SC03 |
| Cross-view | Fills in each camera view from the others | Withhold evidence from a judge, have it predict that evidence, then check the prediction | WP03, SC01 |
| Test-time | Stops a rollout where the generated frames drift from the actions | Spans where a judge's claims stay backed, and spans where they do not | SC04 |

SC3-Eval also anchors its scores to real robot outcomes (Pearson 0.929 across seven policies). The lab's equivalent anchor is agreement with held-out human judgments on real cases. That needs the first human cases and is not part of tonight's run.

The decision-relevance router (SC02) decides which of these checks are worth running at all.
