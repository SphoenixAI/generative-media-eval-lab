# Evaluation methods, standards, and an ontology that preserves intent

Research cutoff and access date: **2026-10-06**. This is a research and design assessment of the supplied master brief, not a certification or an empirical validation of a video evaluator. The initial workspace contained no implementation to assess; statements below compare the brief's proposed methods. The companion `standards-sources.json` records 20 source groups, including pinned paper versions and related official documents. Recommendations are proposed design choices unless explicitly attributed to a source.

## Decision

Keep the brief's backend-first, dimension-level, evidence-first design and Phase 4 stop condition. Its largest gaps are statistical specification, evidence sufficiency, leakage controls, explicit intent and authority, and measured workflow value. A swarm can review an implementation; agreement among agents cannot approve its scientific validity. Approve a deterministic pilot only after its contract and adversarial checks pass. Human field validity remains unestablished until an independent media study is run.

The first useful task should be narrowly defined: **help a production reviewer find consequential generated-video failures and choose a justified next action faster, without increasing missed critical failures**. Compare three conditions: an existing manual checklist, a single evaluator with that checklist, and the bounded specialist system. Improvements in number of graph edges, tests, or agents are not the outcome.

## Concrete changes to the master brief

1. **Sections 7 and 17: treat 0–4 as ordinal.** Retain behavioral anchors. Prefer category distributions, pass rate, catastrophic-failure rate, and paired preferences. Report rate changes in percentage points with denominators. A movement from score 2 to 3 does not establish a 50% quality improvement. A mean score can be a clearly labeled descriptive convenience if justified, but it must not silently become a ratio-scale quality metric.
2. **Sections 5, 8 and 13: separate a missing judgment from a failure.** `SCORED` requires an integer 0–4; `CANNOT_DETERMINE`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `MEDIA_UNAVAILABLE`, and `NOT_ASSIGNED` require a null score and reason. Zero means an observed catastrophic failure. Pairwise `TIE` means judged equivalence under the stated objective; `CANNOT_DETERMINE` means insufficient basis for that comparison. Neither implies both outputs passed.
3. **Sections 8 and 18: specify the statistical instrument.** Persist metric name, distance/weight function, implementation version, exact item/rater population, exclusions, resampling unit and seed. “Alpha calculated” is insufficient.
4. **Sections 5, 15 and 30: add purpose and authority.** The existing `PromptSpec` records requested content; it does not fully capture the creator's intended audience, acceptable tradeoffs, production constraints, decision owner or permitted agent actions. Make those relationships queryable and versioned.
5. **Sections 8 and 16: split the gold set by use.** Separate public teaching examples, rubric development, calibration, fixed hidden audit, and refresh/challenge sets. A published teaching example cannot also be a protected unseen audit item. Revoking an item's audit status after exposure preserves its historical role without pretending it remains unseen.
6. **Sections 9–12 and 23: distinguish policy from physical fact.** A catastrophic geometry score can cause `REGENERATE` under a named production policy; it does not prove that no VFX artist could repair the shot. Record repair budget, shot purpose, available source material, expected labor and confidence. Surreal or deliberately impossible motion can satisfy an explicit creative intention.
7. **Sections 13 and 20–21: enforce blindness at the server.** Before an eligible submission, the response must omit model identity, human consensus, adjudication and automated results. Hiding those values with CSS is not blinding. Record whether a rater has already seen the revealed case; subsequent ratings require a separate analysis stratum.
8. **Sections 18 and 23: never use consensus as the truth oracle.** Reference-match judge-human and human-human comparisons. Keep an independent adjudication process and unresolved-label distributions. The [September revision of the judge-consensus study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03043v2) found that judges' mutual agreement can coexist with systematic disagreement with humans; its evidence is from Indic-language text tasks, not generated video.

## Human evaluation and agreement

Use one unit definition per analysis: **asset × rubric version × dimension**, or **comparison pair × objective × rubric version** for preferences. Do not treat timestamps, extracted frames, dimensions, repeated votes or alternative encodes of one clip as independent videos. Collect raters' judgments independently before discussion. Training examples, practice rounds, reveal exposure, device/playback conditions and specialist experience belong in the evaluation protocol.

For a pilot, independently double-code every selected clip and route disputes to a third reviewer. This is a planning choice, not a universal sample-size requirement. Determine the larger study size from expected event prevalence, acceptable interval width, within-prompt correlation and the critical-failure risk budget. A small fixture collection verifies software; it cannot establish rare-failure recall or representativeness.

Report exact percent agreement alongside a chance-corrected measure and marginal score distributions. Use weighted Cohen's kappa for **two specified raters**, with the weighting policy explicit; retain unweighted kappa for genuinely nominal labels. Kappa changes with category prevalence and marginal distributions, so one coefficient cannot replace confusion matrices and category support. For multiple raters or incomplete rating matrices, ordinal Krippendorff alpha is appropriate when its assumptions and distance are specified. It tolerates absent observations computationally; it does not correct biased participation or selective abstention. These requirements follow the reliability-data principles in [Hayes and Krippendorff](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/2021-03/Answering%20the%20Call%20for%20a%20Standard%20Reliability%20Measure%20for%20Coding%20Data.pdf).

Important implementation distinction: Krippendorff's [computing note](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/2021-03/Computing%20Krippendorff%27s%20Alpha-Reliability.pdf) uses an ordinal distance derived from cumulative marginal frequencies, whereas squared numeric score differences are interval distances. Another [published package description](https://journal.r-project.org/articles/RJ-2021-046/) presents an equidistant-rank ordinal option. These choices are not numerically interchangeable. For this lab, name the canonical method `krippendorff_alpha_ordinal_frequency` and test it against the original worked example: nominal approximately 0.743, ordinal 0.815, interval 0.849. Record any alternative as a different instrument.

For alpha, remove units with fewer than two usable ratings from the coincidence calculation and count them as excluded. Reject duplicate ratings by the same rater for the same unit unless a repeat-measure protocol explicitly separates rounds. Return `null` plus a diagnostic when there is insufficient pairable data or zero expected disagreement; this is a deliberate reporting convention because degenerate-data conventions differ across sources and software. Do not report a degenerate all-identical sample as strong evidence that the rubric discriminates failures. Negative coefficients are legitimate outcomes and must not be clamped away.

An agreement result needs uncertainty and coverage. Bootstrap independent sampled units or their higher-level prompt clusters, retaining all related ratings together. A crossed-rater design may require a crossed bootstrap or hierarchical model if generalizing to a new population of raters. A prompt-only bootstrap supports inference conditional on the observed rater pool. Do not resample every frame or rating independently. Avoid universal “alpha above 0.8 means approved” logic: predeclare application-specific thresholds, consequences and minimum sample support, and show whether intervals cross them.

Video viewing has its own measurement requirements. The current official record lists [ITU-T P.910 (07/2026)](https://www.itu.int/ITU-T/recommendations/rec.aspx?lang=en&rec=16861), approved 2026-07-29, as in force; the [10/2023 edition](https://www.itu.int/ITU-T/recommendations/rec.aspx?id=15697&lang=en) is superseded. The older edition's public summary describes non-interactive subjective multimedia quality assessment. The new edition's clause changes were not inspected, so this report makes no edition-specific conformance claim. As lab design choices, use controlled presentation, documented conditions, training and standardized instructions, and validate custom prompt-fidelity, causal-physics and editability rubrics separately. Public browser votes should be labeled an opportunistic sample and analyzed separately from calibrated studio reviewers.

## Pairwise comparison and regression

Store A, B, tie and cannot-determine separately, plus side order, objective and candidate identities behind the blind mapping. Analyze cannot-determine frequency as a coverage outcome. A three-way tie model can preserve equivalence judgments if the project later needs a ranking; [Ameli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18407v2) develop tie-aware ranking models. Do not turn all ties into half-wins without declaring that convention, and never code abstentions as half-wins. A single global leaderboard is unnecessary for the Phase 4 demo.

For a model comparison, pre-register prompt family, generation settings, target population, dimensions, metric direction, practically important effect and any hard gate. Match outputs by prompt family and generate repeated samples if the claim is about a stochastic generator rather than one lucky clip. Reusing a seed across vendors does not make their outputs equivalent. Keep baseline/candidate pairs together when estimating uncertainty; [Dror et al.](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/) explain why the statistical test must match the evaluation design.

Recommended primary endpoints are prompt-level pass and critical-failure rates with paired differences. For a higher-is-better endpoint, a confirmed decline beyond tolerance requires evidence that the delta is below the predeclared negative margin; a noninferiority pass requires its lower confidence bound above that margin. An inconclusive interval is `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, not automatically no regression. For critical failures, preserve every observed sentinel event and apply the named release policy even if a small sample cannot establish population frequency. Predeclare multiplicity handling when many dimensions or subgroups can trigger statistical claims; exploratory slices remain labeled exploratory.

These choices are conservative design proposals for this lab. The cited NLP statistics paper does not by itself prove that one bootstrap scheme is correct for this video's crossed sampling design.

## Evaluating the evaluator, leakage and abstention

Self-reported confidence is a raw model output until tested. Keep `reported_confidence`, `calibrated_probability`, `calibration_target`, `calibration_version` and `coverage` separate. The probability target must be explicit, such as “matches adjudicated pass/fail under rubric 1.2.” Use held-out reliability plots, Brier/log loss when applicable, and false-negative rates for critical classes. Calibration of a five-category distribution is different from calibration of a binary pass/fail decision. [Guo et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) establish the importance of probability calibration but their temperature-scaling result does not justify treating a generated confidence sentence as logits.

Abstention should trade automation coverage for measured error, with the tradeoff visible. [Selective classification](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html) provides this risk-versus-coverage framing. Use hard evidence/capability checks before any learned threshold: unavailable video, inadequate temporal sampling, unknown rubric, out-of-domain input, inaccessible timestamp, missing critical dimension or malformed evaluator response all force review. A high confidence field cannot override these checks.

The [24 September 2026 conversation-judge paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29431v1) supports keeping calibration anchors and a function version separate from the judge. It does not show transfer to video judgments or eliminate positional bias. [Fiedler's May 2026 analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06939v1) further warns that reusing calibration across compared models can yield misleading differences if the judge's error behavior changes. Therefore recalibrate or at least audit by generator family, content category and evaluator update before reusing an old calibration map.

Freeze the split before prompt optimization, evaluator tuning or gold selection. Group related prompt templates, near-duplicate clips and derived encodes within the same split. Never feed hidden audit answers into agent memory, retrieval, demonstrations, public case payloads or a reviewer prompt that later becomes judge configuration. Record exposure history. Public-web retrieval can leak answers even without training contamination, as shown in [Search-Time Data Contamination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13180v1); reserve a closed-book judging mode for protected audits and log every external retrieval for retrieval-enabled protocols.

A human-labeled disagreement item can be valuable without one universal gold score. Represent a label distribution or allowed set, adjudication policy and scope. Do not select the gold set only from high-agreement easy cases and then claim hard-case competence. Track fixed canaries for reproducibility, an untouched audit for independent measurement, and rolling challenge sets for newly observed failures; these serve different purposes.

## Standards and their limits

The [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) and [Generative AI Profile, AI 600-1](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1) support lifecycle governance, contextual risk assessment, measurement and management. Map evidence records and reviewer responsibilities to these functions, without claiming NIST certification. The RMF page says version 1.0 is being revised; a revision process is not a replacement release.

The newer [NIST AI 200-2 TEVV-Athlon announcement](https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence/ai-research/tevv-athlon-framework-evaluating-ai-systems) is especially relevant: it describes an extensible approach to assessing AI systems' real-world outcomes, including multimodal and agentic systems. As of this cutoff it is an **initial public draft**, announced 2026-08-07 with comments closing 2026-10-06. Use it as a design cross-check, not as a finalized standard or a compliance badge.

[ISO/IEC 42001:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001) concerns an organization's AI management system. [ISO/IEC 23894:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/77304.html) supplies AI risk-management guidance. [ISO/IEC 25059:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/80655.html) is a quality-model reference; its catalog reports a replacement FDIS in development. Their public scopes help organize governance and quality requirements. This research did not perform a licensed clause-by-clause audit, and implementing typed records does not certify the organization or product.

[PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/) provides reusable entity, activity, agent, plan, role, derivation and delegation concepts. [SHACL](https://www.w3.org/TR/shacl/) validates graph constraints. [OWL 2](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-primer/) expresses ontology semantics under an open-world assumption: absent facts are not automatically false. Use explicit unknown states in the application rather than expecting the ontology to fill missing observations. Structural conformance and logical consistency cannot establish factual correctness.

[C2PA 2.4](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/specs/C2PA_Specification.html) can record cryptographically bound provenance; its official change history dates 2.4 to April 2026. The [official explainer](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/explainer/Explainer.html) distinguishes provenance from factual truth and acknowledges incomplete or removable metadata. Keep credential states such as `PRESENT_VALIDATED`, `PRESENT_INVALID`, `ABSENT` and `NOT_CHECKED`; absence is not a synthetic-media verdict, and a valid signature is not evidence that a scene's physics or claims are true. Integration is an optional ingest adapter, not a prerequisite for a useful Phase 4 lab.

## Intent-aware relational ontology

The minimal extension should use relational tables and typed records now; preserve stable identifiers for a later RDF/JSON-LD projection. A graph database and a heavyweight reasoner are not prerequisites.

Add these versioned types:

- **IntentSpec**: principal/author, original statement, intended audience and use, objective IDs, acceptable tradeoffs, constraint IDs, provenance, approval state and superseded version.
- **Objective**: desired outcome, beneficiary, success predicate, priority, applicable context and observation window. “Ship a believable walking shot” and “depict intentional surreal motion” are different objectives.
- **Constraint**: hard/soft classification, affected action or artifact, limit/value/unit, rationale, scope, source and authority. Budget and duration are constraints; an evaluator's confidence is not authorization.
- **AuthorityGrant**: principal, delegated agent/role, permitted actions and resources, limits, expiry/revocation and evidence of approval. A recommendation and an executed production change are different events.
- **RelationAssertion**: subject, predicate, object, relation kind, author/asserter, evidence references, epistemic status, confidence kind, valid time, recorded time, context and version. Retraction appends a new record.
- **EvidenceObservation**: asset checksum, source locator, time interval/frame sampling, extraction method/version, measured value and units, observer, availability and integrity status. Predictions are never stored as completed observations.
- **DecisionRecord**: alternatives, objective, applicable constraints, supporting/contradicting claims, policy version, decision owner, authority, next action and unresolved uncertainty.

Useful predicate distinctions include `intends`, `requires`, `permits`, `violates`, `supports`, `contradicts`, `derived_from`, `predicts`, `tests`, `suggests_cause`, `authorized_by`, `performed_by` and `observed_result`. Do not compress them into a generic `related_to` edge. Separate epistemic status (`DECLARED`, `OBSERVED`, `INFERRED`, `HYPOTHETICAL`, `DISPUTED`, `RETRACTED`) from normative status (`REQUIRED`, `PREFERRED`, `PERMITTED`, `PROHIBITED`). Inferred user intention remains a proposal until the authorized human confirms it or a preexisting explicit instruction supports it.

Example: a creator **declares** an objective to preserve a subject's identity while depicting impossible motion. A requirement **permits** the unusual trajectory but **requires** identity continuity. A timecoded observation **supports** identity drift. The physics specialist's general plausibility complaint does not override the declared artistic exception. A repair proposal **predicts** restored identity, is bounded by a labor constraint, and only becomes an executed intervention after authority is established. The result is linked to a new observation rather than inferred from the agent's completion message.

The [2026 TIO-SHACL preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27359v1) demonstrates formal validation of intent structures in telecommunications. Transfer the pattern of validating constraints and conflicting expectations; do not import its domain wholesale or claim it has validated creative evaluation. PROV's plan and qualified association can map the method and the responsible agent, while application types carry measurable outcomes and constraints.

A hypothesis graph is not automatically a causal graph. Multiple camera/scene mechanisms can produce indistinguishable rendered observations. Label causal claims `UNIDENTIFIED` until a discriminating intervention or defensible identification assumptions are recorded. For product-engagement cases, confounding, selection and instrumentation can prevent a causal conclusion even with many connections. [Hernán and Robins](https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/miguel-hernan/wp-content/uploads/sites/1268/2024/04/hernanrobins_WhatIf_26apr24.pdf) explain the distinction between association and identifiable causal effects. Interventions should specify expected observations and falsifiers; numerical hypothesis confidence without a probabilistic update model should be labeled an assessment, not a posterior probability.

## Minimal sustainable layers and human workflow

1. **Versioned evidence store**: typed core records, content hashes, append-only audit, explicit split and exposure history. One backend serves private/public/embed projections.
2. **Measurement instruments**: rubric anchors, dimension applicability, deterministic checks, metric definitions and uncertainty. Judge adapters produce observations and claims through the same contracts.
3. **Bounded orchestration**: independent specialist passes, finite budgets, dependency-aware scheduling, exact-input cache, cancellation, retry limits, no self-issued authority. Re-run only instruments affected by an artifact or version change.
4. **Decision policy**: evidence sufficiency, critical-failure gates, calibrated abstention and escalation. Preserve disagreements for a human decision packet.
5. **Validation and governance**: hidden audit, metamorphic/adversarial tests, drift monitoring, source refresh and change review. New models enter shadow evaluation before they can affect release decisions.

Agents prepare ingestion manifests, frame evidence, rubric suggestions, candidate failure tags, deterministic diagnostics and comparison packets. A human selects the real use objective, resolves material conflicting constraints, adjudicates ambiguous or consequential judgments, approves rubric/gold changes and controls costly or externally visible actions. Routine evidence processing continues within the authorized envelope. Review packets contain the exact evidence, competing interpretations, required decision, estimated effort and expected effect, not a long transcript of agent discussion.

[Husain and Shankar's practitioner guidance](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/whats-a-minimum-viable-evaluation-setup.html), updated May 2026, recommends beginning with direct error analysis of a small set of traces. Treat that as experienced practice, not a controlled demonstration that one reviewer is statistically sufficient. Use one accountable product owner for intent and policy, alongside independent raters for reliability measurement.

Measure active reviewer minutes per case, time to first substantiated critical finding, missed critical failures, unsupported flags, escalation rate, reviewer override rate, rework, dollars per completed evaluation and resolved cases per hour. Compare the three baseline conditions on the same held-out task families with counterbalanced assignment to reduce learning effects. Predeclare a usefulness target only after baseline timing and error rates are known. Maximal automation coverage is not the objective if it increases costly misses.

## Test approval checklist for the Phase 4 checkpoint

These are proposed acceptance checks, not claims that they have run:

- Domain contracts reject bool-as-score, out-of-range scores, nonfinite confidence, a score paired with abstention, missing critical evidence and dangling references.
- Alpha matches published nontrivial fixtures and differs from interval alpha on unequal frequency data; includes missingness, negative agreement, one-rater units and degenerate distributions.
- Duplicate raters, leaked reveal data, wrong rubric versions and contradictory intent constraints are rejected or explicitly routed.
- Reversing A/B presentation maps answers back to the same underlying candidates; tie and cannot-determine survive serialization distinctly.
- A missing specialist, timeout, malformed result or unsupported modality yields incomplete/review status, never a passing aggregate.
- A critical failure cannot be averaged away. A declared style exception applies only to its stated dimension/context.
- Replaying the same fixture, policy and versions reproduces the deterministic result. Changing the rubric, frames, model/configuration, objective or calibration invalidates relevant cached results.
- A red-team fixture gives every agent the same wrong claim; the harness must still demand independent evidence. A swarm majority is not sufficient approval.
- Public and embed results expose only eligible curated evidence, and anonymous users cannot trigger paid execution or mutate rubrics and gold sets.
- Empirical validation remains explicitly `NOT_ESTABLISHED` until real media, independent human ratings, representative sampling and a competent baseline comparison exist.

## Search log and independent fact-check sweep

Initial discovery used query families for Krippendorff/ordinal/missing data, NIST generative-AI profile, ITU subjective video assessment, ISO AI standards, W3C provenance/shapes/OWL, C2PA truth limits, calibration/abstention, paired statistical testing and leakage. The recency sweep added 2026 judge calibration, consensus/human alignment, intent ontology validation and NIST TEVV drafts. Primary pages, author-hosted papers and version histories were opened after search discovery; practitioner writing was labeled separately. Search was broad but cannot prove complete coverage of the internet or publications later in October.

Fact-check outcomes:

- **Corrected current edition after independent agent review**: the initial report cited P.910 (10/2023); another researcher identified the current 07/2026 edition. Reopening both official catalog records confirmed 2026-07-29 approval and the older edition's superseded status. The new edition's detailed clauses remain uninspected.
- **Corrected date**: a secondary search result dated C2PA 2.4 to September; the official specification says April 2026.
- **Corrected paper version**: search summarized the June consensus paper's 41-judge version; the current September v2 reports 42. Recommendations cite v2 and avoid transporting its effect sizes to video.
- **Corrected normative status**: NIST TEVV-Athlon is an initial public draft; ISO 25059's announced replacement is still under development. Neither was promoted to a final new standard.
- **Resolved formula ambiguity**: inspected Krippendorff's original computing note after finding a package paper's alternative ordinal-distance convention. The lab should pin the original frequency-based variant and test published examples.
- **Separated calibration claims**: the newest conversation calibration result is not a video validation; probability calibration is not equivalent to self-reported confidence; shared calibration can fail across model families.
- **Rejected unsupported inference**: no standards source establishes that provenance proves truth, a graph proves causality, unanimous agents prove correctness or synthetic fixture success proves human usefulness.
- **Unresolved empirical questions**: optimal video sampling policy, dimension separability, rater agreement, calibration transfer, VFX cost accuracy and actual reviewer speed remain hypotheses for the next human/media study.

The literature supports this testing structure; it does not yet approve the system's effectiveness. Phase 4 approval should mean **bounded software pilot ready for empirical testing**, contingent on the repository's actual test evidence, with later-phase features kept as contracts and documented next work.
